# A101198: partitions of n with rank 1

`src/wolfram/A101198.wl` is a Wolfram Language package (context `OEIS`) for [A101198](https://oeis.org/A101198), the number of partitions of n with rank 1. Dyson defined the rank of a partition as its largest part minus its number of parts. The package counts partitions of any rank m, so it also gives [A047993](https://oeis.org/A047993) (rank 0), [A101199](https://oeis.org/A101199) (rank 2), [A101200](https://oeis.org/A101200) (rank 3), [A363213](https://oeis.org/A363213) (rank 4), [A363214](https://oeis.org/A363214) (rank 5), and in general the columns of the triangle [A063995](https://oeis.org/A063995) of partitions of n by rank.

**Notation.** $N(m, n)$ is the number of partitions of $n$ with rank $m$, and $p(n)$ the number of all partitions of $n$, `PartitionsP[n]` in Wolfram. $(x)_k = (1 - x)(1 - x^2)\cdots(1 - x^k)$.

## The sequence

The terms for $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$ begin

```
0, 1, 0, 1, 1, 2, 1, 3, 3, 5, 5, 8, 8, 13, 14, 20, 23, 31, 35, 48, ...
```

For example $a(8) = 3$: the partitions 5+1+1+1, 4+3+1 and 4+2+2 each have a largest part one more than their number of parts. The OEIS entry gives the asymptotic $a(n) \sim \dfrac{\pi\thinspace e^{\pi\sqrt{2n/3}}}{3 \cdot 2^{9/2}\thinspace n^{3/2}}$ (Kotesovec); the ratio of $a(n)$ to it is 0.971, 0.991 and 0.997 at $n = 100$, $1000$ and $10000$.

## Loading and calling

From the repository root:

```wolfram
Get["src/wolfram/A101198.wl"];
A101198[8]                       (* 3 *)
PartitionRankCountList[1, 20]    (* the 20 terms above *)
PartitionRankCount[3, 20]        (* 40, the 20th term of A101200 *)
PartitionRankCount[-3, 20]       (* 40 as well: conjugation reverses the rank *)
A101198 /@ {100, 1000}           (* {6203548, 245302742607816804169710132801} *)
```

## Public functions

| Call | Returns |
|---|---|
| `A101198[n]` | The number of partitions of $n$ with rank 1. |
| `PartitionRankCount[m, n]` | $N(m, n)$, the number of partitions of $n$ with rank $m$, for any integer $m$. |
| `PartitionRankCountList[m, nmax]` | $\lbrace N(m, 1), \dots, N(m, n_{\max})\rbrace$. |

`n` and `nmax` are positive integers and `m` is an integer. Otherwise the function gives a message (`::posint` or `::int`) and returns `$Failed`. Like the OEIS entries, the sequences start at $n = 1$, so the empty partition is not counted.

## How it works

1. **Dyson's generating function.** For $m \ge 0$,

   $$\sum_{n \ge 1} N(m, n)\thinspace x^n = \frac{\sum_{k \ge 1} (-1)^{k-1} x^{k(3k-1)/2 + mk} (1 - x^k)}{\prod_{r \ge 1} (1 - x^r)}$$

   This is equation (2.12) of Atkin and Swinnerton-Dyer (1954), who credit it to Dyson (1944) and prove it as their Lemma 1. Ranks below 0 follow from $N(-m, n) = N(m, n)$: conjugating a partition (transposing its Ferrers diagram) swaps the largest part and the number of parts.
2. **Why it holds (Lemma 1).** A partition of $n$ with $s$ parts and rank $m \ge 0$ has largest part $s + m$. Removing it leaves a partition of $n - s - m$ into exactly $s - 1$ parts, each at most $s + m$. Two identities of Cauchy (Hardy and Wright, Theorems 348 and 349, with $\left[{N \atop u}\right]$ the Gaussian binomial $\prod_{t=1}^{u} \frac{1 - x^{N-t+1}}{1 - x^t}$)

   $$\prod_{v=1}^{j} (1 + a x^v) = \sum_{u=0}^{j} a^u x^{u(u+1)/2} \left[{j \atop u}\right]$$

   $$\prod_{v=1}^{j} (1 - a x^v)^{-1} = \sum_{u \ge 0} a^u x^u \left[{j+u-1 \atop u}\right]$$

   turn the count for each $s$ into an alternating sum over $u$. After summing over $s$ and swapping the two sums, the inner sum over $s$ is $x^{u^2} \sum_k x^{k(u+1)} / (x)_k$. By Euler's identity $\sum_k a^k x^k / (x)_k = \prod_{v \ge 1} 1/(1 - a x^v)$ (the second identity as $j \to \infty$) with $a = x^u$, this is $x^{u^2} / \prod_{r > u} (1 - x^r)$, which cancels the product $\prod_{r > u} (1 - x^r)$ in front of it. What is left of the $u$-th term is $(-1)^{u-1} x^{u(u-1)/2 + mu + u^2} (1 - x^u)$, and $u(u - 1)/2 + u^2 = u(3u - 1)/2$ gives (2.12). Each Cauchy identity follows by comparing coefficients of $a^u$ in a functional equation, $(1 + ax) F(ax) = (1 + a x^{j+1}) F(a)$ for the first product $F$ and $(1 - ax) G(a) = (1 - a x^{j+1}) G(ax)$ for the second $G$, which gives a one-step recurrence for the coefficients.
3. **One term.** Expanding $1/\prod (1 - x^r) = \sum p(j)\thinspace x^j$ and reading off the coefficient of $x^n$:

   $$N(m, n) = \sum_{k \ge 1} (-1)^{k-1} \left[ p\negthinspace \left(n - \tfrac{k(3k-1)}{2} - mk\right) - p\negthinspace \left(n - \tfrac{k(3k+1)}{2} - mk\right) \right]$$

   with $p(j) = 0$ for $j < 0$. The sum stops once $k(3k - 1)/2 + mk > n$, so it has at most about $\sqrt{2n/3}$ terms. For rank 1 the offsets are $k(3k+1)/2$ and $3k(k+1)/2$:

   $$a(n) = p(n-2) - p(n-3) - p(n-7) + p(n-9) + p(n-15) - p(n-18) - \cdots$$

   `A101198[n]` is `PartitionRankCount[1, n]`, and `PartitionRankCount` is this sum written with `PartitionsP`, as in

   ```wolfram
   a[n_] := Sum[(-1)^(k-1) (PartitionsP[n - k (3k+1)/2] - PartitionsP[n - 3k (k+1)/2]), {k, 1, Floor[(Sqrt[24 n + 1] - 1)/6] + 1}]
   ```

   `PartitionRankCountList` is a `Table` of these.

**Garden of Eden partitions.** Summing (2.12) over $m \ge 2$ telescopes, since $\sum_{m \ge 2} x^{mk} = x^{2k}/(1 - x^k)$, to $\sum_{m \ge 2} N(m, n) = \sum_{k \ge 1} (-1)^{k+1} p\negthinspace \left(n - \tfrac{3k(k+1)}{2}\right)$. By conjugation this counts the partitions with rank at most $-2$, which are the Garden of Eden partitions of Bulgarian solitaire, and it is the Hopkins–Sellers formula behind `BulgarianSolitaireGardenOfEdenCount` (see [Bulgarian solitaire](BulgarianSolitaire.md)).

## Checks

Run `test/wolfram/A101198.wlt`. It checks:

- the examples on this page;
- the DATA sections of the OEIS entries for ranks 0 to 5 (A047993, A101198, A101199, A101200, A363213, A363214);
- values from the A101198 b-file at $n = 100$, $500$ and $1000$;
- every $N(m, n)$ with $|m| \le 6$ and $n \le 24$ against a count of `IntegerPartitions`;
- that the counts over all ranks add up to $p(n)$, the extreme ranks $\pm(n - 1)$, and the Hopkins–Sellers sum for ranks $\ge 2$;
- the messages for invalid arguments.
