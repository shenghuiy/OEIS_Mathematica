# A363214: partitions of n with rank 5

[A363214](https://oeis.org/A363214) is the number of partitions of n with rank 5, where the rank of a partition is its largest part minus its number of parts. It has no package of its own: it is `PartitionRankCount[5, n]` from `src/wolfram/A101198.wl`, the package for rank 1. The derivation, the proof of the generating function and the timings are on the [A101198](A101198.md) page; this page gives what is specific to rank 5.

**Notation.** As on the A101198 page, $N(m, n)$ is the number of partitions of $n$ with rank $m$ and $p(n)$ is `PartitionsP[n]`.

## The sequence

The terms for $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$ begin

```
0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 1, 0, 1, 1, 2, 2, 4, 4, 7, 7, 11, 13, 19, 21, 30, ...
```

For example $a(12) = 4$: the partitions 9+1+1+1, 8+3+1, 8+2+2 and 7+5 each have a largest part five more than their number of parts. The first nonzero term is $a(6) = 1$, from the single part 6. By conjugation, $a(n)$ also counts the partitions of $n$ with rank $-5$.

The OEIS entry gives no asymptotic. The one given for ranks 1 and 2, $\dfrac{\pi\, e^{\pi\sqrt{2n/3}}}{3 \cdot 2^{9/2}\, n^{3/2}}$, fits numerically: the ratio of $a(n)$ to it is 0.880, 0.982 and 0.996 at $n = 100$, $1000$ and $10000$. It approaches 1 more slowly as the rank grows (compare 0.971, 0.959, 0.939 and 0.912 at $n = 100$ for ranks 1 to 4).

## The formula for rank 5

Dyson's rank generating function, equation (2.12) of Atkin and Swinnerton-Dyer (1954), with $m = 5$:

$$\sum_{n \ge 1} N(5, n)\, x^n = \frac{\sum_{k \ge 1} (-1)^{k-1} x^{3k(k+3)/2} (1 - x^k)}{\prod_{r \ge 1} (1 - x^r)}$$

since $k(3k - 1)/2 + 5k = 3k(k + 3)/2$. This is the generating function in the OEIS entry, written there as $x^{5k} \left(x^{k(3k-1)/2} - x^{k(3k+1)/2}\right)$. Reading off the coefficient of $x^n$, the two offsets are $3k(k+3)/2$ and $k(3k+11)/2$:

$$a(n) = p(n-6) - p(n-7) - p(n-15) + p(n-17) + p(n-27) - p(n-30) - \cdots$$

with $p(j) = 0$ for $j < 0$. As for [rank 2](A101199.md#the-formula-for-rank-2), the two halves of the sum count the partitions with rank at least 5 and at least 6, so $a(n)$ is their difference. The first offsets $3k(k+3)/2 = 6, 15, 27, \dots$ are the second offsets of the [rank-4](A363213.md#the-formula-for-rank-4) formula.

For one term, `PartitionRankCount[5, n]` evaluates the sum above for $n \le 1000$ and uses the series division of `PartitionRankCountList` above that, as described in [Performance](A101198.md#performance).

## Checks

`test/wolfram/A101198.wlt` checks the examples on this page, the OEIS DATA section of A363214 (58 terms), and every $N(5, n)$ with $n \le 24$ against a count of `IntegerPartitions`. In addition, `PartitionRankCountList[5, 10000]` was compared with all 10,000 values of the OEIS b-file (Seiichi Manyama), with no mismatches.
