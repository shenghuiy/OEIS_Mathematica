# A101200: partitions of n with rank 3

[A101200](https://oeis.org/A101200) is the number of partitions of n with rank 3, where the rank of a partition is its largest part minus its number of parts. It has no package of its own: it is `PartitionRankCount[3, n]` from `src/wolfram/A101198.wl`, the package for rank 1. The derivation, the proof of the generating function and the timings are on the [A101198](A101198.md) page; this page gives what is specific to rank 3.

**Notation.** As on the A101198 page, $N(m, n)$ is the number of partitions of $n$ with rank $m$ and $p(n)$ is `PartitionsP[n]`.

## The sequence

The terms for $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$ begin

```
0, 0, 0, 1, 0, 1, 1, 2, 2, 4, 3, 6, 7, 10, 11, 17, 18, 26, 30, 40, ...
```

For example $a(11) = 3$: the partitions 7+2+1+1, 6+4+1 and 6+3+2 each have a largest part three more than their number of parts. The first nonzero term is $a(4) = 1$, from the single part 4. By conjugation, $a(n)$ also counts the partitions of $n$ with rank $-3$.

The OEIS entry gives no asymptotic. The one given for ranks 1 and 2, $\dfrac{\pi\thinspace e^{\pi\sqrt{2n/3}}}{3 \cdot 2^{9/2}\thinspace n^{3/2}}$, fits numerically: the ratio of $a(n)$ to it is 0.939, 0.988 and 0.997 at $n = 100$, $1000$ and $10000$.

Get["src/wolfram/A101198.wl"];

## The formula for rank 3

Dyson's rank generating function, equation (2.12) of Atkin and Swinnerton-Dyer (1954), with $m = 3$:

$$\sum_{n \ge 1} N(3, n)\thinspace x^n = \frac{\sum_{k \ge 1} (-1)^{k-1} x^{k(3k+5)/2} (1 - x^k)}{\prod_{r \ge 1} (1 - x^r)}$$

since $k(3k - 1)/2 + 3k = k(3k + 5)/2$. Reading off the coefficient of $x^n$, the two offsets are $k(3k+5)/2$ and $k(3k+7)/2$:

$$a(n) = p(n-4) - p(n-5) - p(n-11) + p(n-13) + p(n-21) - p(n-24) - \cdots$$

with $p(j) = 0$ for $j < 0$. As for [rank 2](A101199.md#the-formula-for-rank-2), the two halves of the sum count the partitions with rank at least 3 and at least 4, so $a(n)$ is their difference. The first offsets $k(3k+5)/2 = 4, 11, 21, \dots$ are the second offsets of the rank-2 formula.

For one term, `PartitionRankCount[3, n]` evaluates the sum above for $n \le 1000$ and uses the series division of `PartitionRankCountList` above that, as described in [Performance](A101198.md#performance).

## Checks

`test/wolfram/A101198.wlt` checks the examples on this page, the OEIS DATA section of A101200 (58 terms), and every $N(3, n)$ with $n \le 24$ against a count of `IntegerPartitions`. In addition, `PartitionRankCountList[3, 1000]` was compared with all 1,000 values of the OEIS b-file (Seiichi Manyama), with no mismatches.
